# Audience and page map

Who the site has to convince, what each of them needs to read, and which page does it.
The personas are working assumptions drawn from the planning notes, not from interviews.
The open questions at the end are the ones that would change the site.

## Who is reading

### 1. The owner-doctor (the buyer)

A podiatrist in Arizona who owns a solo practice or is a partner in a group. Both are
targets. Clinically confident, short on
time, not trained in business. Has been pitched by agencies, software reps and private
equity, and is wary of all three.

- **Thinks in symptoms, not service categories.** "My phones go to voicemail", not
  "operations".
- **Fears:** losing control, paying for a report that sits on a shelf, staff revolt,
  a long contract.
- **Needs to see:** their own problem named, what it costs to start, that the practice
  stays theirs, and proof it worked for someone like them.

### 2. The practice manager (the gatekeeper)

Often the person who fills out the questionnaire and who lives with the changes. Can
quietly stall an engagement. Hears "optimization" as "you have been doing it wrong" or
"jobs are at risk".

- **Needs to see:** that this is done with the team, that staffing is not on the table,
  and that work comes off their desk.

### 3. The comparison shopper

A doctor already in a coaching program, already paying an agency, or weighing an MSO
offer. Asks "how is this different from what I have?"

### 4. Segments that change the message

- **Insurance-based vs cash-pay.** Collections means copays up front for one and payment
  at time of service for the other. The site currently mentions both in one line.
- **Solo vs multi-provider.** A solo doctor is the buyer and the bottleneck. A group has
  partners to align and usually a manager.

## Biggest gaps

1. **No people.** A doctor is hiring partners, and the site shows no one. An About page
   with the founders' names, faces and relevant background is the most important
   missing page.
2. **No proof.** No case study, testimonial or number. Competitors lead with these.
3. **No sample deliverable.** The before and after maps are the core of the pitch and
   the site only describes them. A redacted or illustrative example on `/discovery`
   would do more than any paragraph.
4. **No ongoing price.** The discovery fee invites the next question.

## Open questions

- Answered: Mint starts in Arizona and targets both solo and group practices. The site
  now says so. Which Arizona metros come first is still open.
- Who usually makes first contact, the doctor or the manager?
- Is the first wave insurance-based podiatry, or does it include cash-heavy practices?
- Which three specialties follow podiatry?
- Who are the partners, and what are their backgrounds?
