# Competitive landscape

Researched November 2026 from public web sources. Almost everything below comes from
the vendors' own pages, press releases and review directories, so treat performance
claims as unverified. No competitor publishes consulting or coaching prices; the price
points listed are the few that were public. Nobody was contacted for a quote.

## The short version

No one found sells quite what Mint sells: an evaluation, then execution, across
operations, revenue and marketing, without ownership. Practices instead buy the pieces
from five kinds of vendor. Each will position against Mint differently.

| Category | Examples | What they sell | Public price signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Podiatry coaching | Top Practices (mastermind since 2007, plus a Virtual Practice Management Institute that coaches staff) | Training, peer group, accountability. The practice does the work. | Not published |
| Podiatry marketing agencies | Podiatry Content Connection (PCC), Blue Orchid Marketing, Foster Web Marketing, Podiatry Growth, Healthcare Success | Websites, SEO, ads, reputation, email and SMS | About $500 to $650 a month at the low end; most quote privately |
| General practice consultants | MGMA Consulting, Healthcare Administrative Partners, Curi Advisory, Withum, Practolytics | Assessment and recommendations; some are billing-led | Not published. One benchmark puts healthcare consulting broadly at about $314 an hour |
| Patient communication software | Weave, NexHealth, Tebra | Texting, reminders, reviews, phones | Weave from about $199 to $249 a month, NexHealth about $350, Tebra's patient product listed at $700 |
| MSOs and private equity | Not researched | Management in exchange for control | n/a |

## Closest competitors

**PCC (Podiatry Content Connection)** is the largest podiatry-only marketer found. It
says it serves more than 8% of US podiatry practices with 96% client retention, and
offers a performance guarantee. Its products include Practice Builder (website and
weekly content) and Patient Pro Connect (existing-patient relationships), which overlaps
Mint's remarketing. It does not appear to touch operations or collections.

**Foster Web Marketing** is the nearest in shape: marketing plus coaching, organized as
"four pillars of marketing" (internet, referral, internal, external), with clients moving
from coaching to done-for-you services. Its pillars are all marketing; Mint's three
include operations and revenue.

**Top Practices** owns the podiatry coaching relationship. It teaches doctors and staff
to run the practice better. It is the firm most likely to be already in the room.

**MGMA Consulting** covers the same operational ground Mint's discovery does (patient
flow, front and back office, intake, EMR optimization), but as a classic assessment, and
it also advises on staffing, which Mint deliberately does not.

## How they would compare themselves to Mint

- **"We have results; they have none yet."** PCC cites retention and ROI figures. Mint has
  no case studies. This is the biggest gap.
- **"We only do podiatry."** Podiatry-only firms will call a multi-specialty partner a
  generalist.
- **"We have a community."** Coaching programs offer peers, a summit and years of content.
- **"Our software costs $300 a month."** A practice comparing Mint's fee to a tool's will
  miss that the tool needs staff to run it.
- **"Three pillars is a lot for a small team."** Agencies will say Mint is spread thin.
- **"We publish what it costs."** Mint publishes the $1,500 discovery fee but not the
  monthly fee it rolls into.

## Where Mint is different

- **Execution.** Coaches teach and consultants recommend. Mint does the work.
- **The whole practice.** Agencies create demand; Mint also fixes what happens when the
  phone rings. Marketing spend is wasted if calls go to voicemail.
- **A low-risk first step.** A flat, published $1,500 that rolls into month one. No
  competitor found publishes an entry price for an assessment.
- **A visual deliverable.** Before and after maps, where others deliver a report.
- **Not an MSO.** No ownership, and staffing stays with the practice.

## What this suggests

1. Get two or three pilot results in podiatry and publish them. Numbers are what the
   incumbents lead with.
2. Decide the monthly price, or at least a range. The discovery roll-in raises the
   question immediately.
3. Treat coaching programs and software vendors as referral partners, not rivals. Their
   clients know what to do and lack the hands to do it.
4. Lead with the phone-to-collection gap that agencies leave open.
5. Keep podiatry-specific pages and questionnaire depth so "generalist" does not stick.
